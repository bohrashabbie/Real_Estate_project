Kwt25 · عقار الكويت · Visual identity proposal

A property office that behaves like an architecture studio.

The rejected design was a listings template: white ground, blue-family accent, rounded cards, a stock hero. This is not a re-skin of it. The palette, the type system, the grid, and every component have been rebuilt around one idea — that the buildings are the product, and everything else should get out of their way.

Arabic-first · RTL Light default · dark included 7 screens + 3 mobile Kuwait · د.ك
Position

The one decision everything else follows from: no gold.

Every property brokerage in the Gulf signals premium with gold on navy or gold on cream. Because they all do it, none of them read as premium — the cue has been spent. The old kwt25 site used exactly that combination, which is a large part of why it felt like something the client had already seen.

This identity takes the harder route: luxury signalled through restraint, space, and photography rather than metallic accents. The single accent is the sea-green of the ceramic discs on the Kuwait Towers — a colour that belongs to this city rather than to the category. It appears on perhaps two percent of any screen. That scarcity is what makes it read as expensive.

The ground is plaster, not cream. That distinction is the whole game on a light site: cream plus a metallic is the combination the client rejected, while a cool mineral grey-green with no metallic at all reads as a material rather than a decoration. Try the toggle — the identity is built to survive both grounds, but light is the default and the one tuned first.

Why the accent has two values. Gulf is #2E6B54 on plaster and #5E9B84 on basalt. The same hex on both grounds would fail contrast on one of them — the light version needs depth to hold against a pale ground, the dark version needs lift. Naive inversion is what makes most dual-theme sites feel broken in their second theme.

Photography treatment. Images grade to a duotone between the ground and Gulf, then fade into the page at the bottom edge so cards have no hard cut. Listings arrive from many phone cameras in wildly different light; a fixed grade is what makes a page of mixed sources look like one brand.

02 · Theme

The switch

Light is the default. The control offers three states, not two — because a visitor who has set their phone to dark has expressed a preference, and a site that ignores it is being rude.

StateStampResult
Lightdata-theme="light"Plaster ground, always — beats a dark OS setting.
Darkdata-theme="dark"Basalt ground, always — beats a light OS setting.
Systemno attributeFollows the device. Falls back to light where unknown.

Placement in the product. The toggle sits in the top bar beside the EN / ع language switch, as a matched pair of two-state controls — language and appearance are the same kind of choice and belong together. On mobile both move into the menu sheet.

Persistence. The choice is stored locally and applied before first paint, so a returning visitor never sees a flash of the wrong ground. Every colour in the system resolves through tokens, so the switch is one attribute on the root element — no component knows which theme it is in.

03 · Type

Bilingual type system

Arabic is the design language, not a translation of a Latin layout. Headlines are set in an editorial Arabic with real stroke contrast; the interface is set in a clean geometric Arabic; all numerals, areas, and reference codes sit in a mono face.

04 · Structure

Spacing, radius, elevation

The system's most opinionated values are the ones set to zero. Corners are square and cards cast no shadow — the two habits that make a listings site look like a listings site.

Why zero radius matters commercially. A 12-pixel corner radius is the single strongest visual marker of a templated marketplace — it is what the client recognised when they said "the same as before." Square corners plus a visible hairline grid reads as a drawing sheet rather than a card deck, and that is the whole gap between a portal and a brokerage.

Shadows are theme-dependent, which is why they are tokens. On plaster the sticky shadow is a warm grey at low opacity; on basalt it is black. A single shadow value tuned for one ground turns into a grey smear on the other.

What changed and why

The old home page put a pre-baked marketing banner in the hero and floated the search panel on top of it, which meant the first thing a visitor saw was an advertisement rather than the inventory. Here the hero is split: the value line holds the right half in large editorial Arabic, and real photography holds the left, full-bleed and uncropped by any overlay. The search bar sits below the fold line as its own chalk block, so it is never fighting the artwork for the same pixels — which was the actual cause of the headline and call-to-action being clipped in the old build. For a brokerage pitching to sellers, the page now opens by demonstrating the quality of presentation their property will receive.

What changed and why

The old results page opened with a tall navy banner, then a full-width accordion of every filter, so the first listing sat far below the fold and the filters had to be re-opened on every visit. Here the filters collapse into one sticky bar where applied filters read as removable chips — the state of the search is always visible without occupying a screen of its own. Sold and rented stock stays in the grid with a muted Closed badge rather than being hidden, because a brokerage's recently-transacted inventory is evidence that it sells; a portal hides it, an office shows it.

What changed and why

The old detail page stacked a single image, then a description, then contact buttons far down the page, so the two things a buyer decides on — what it looks like and what it costs — were never on screen together. The gallery now opens as an asymmetric plate group at full width, and price plus the three contact routes sit in a sticky rail that stays with the visitor through the whole scroll. Specs are set as a dimension row rather than icon chips, which is what stopped the labels truncating to "2 bath…" on narrow cards. For the client, WhatsApp is one tap from any scroll position, which is where their leads actually come from.

What changed and why

Smart search was previously a form on a side page — five fields stacked in a box, indistinguishable from the ordinary filters and reached from a menu item most visitors never opened. It is now a full-bleed one-question-per-screen flow with the answers accumulating as a visible list on the photographic half, so progress is legible and the feature feels like the office thinking rather than a form submitting. When it widens a search it says so in plain Arabic naming the relaxed filter, instead of printing a raw budget_max key at the visitor. This is the screen to demo in a pitch — it is the only thing here a competitor's template cannot copy in a week.

What changed and why

The request form was a generic stack of rounded inputs; it now uses the same bordered field grid as the search bar, so the two most important interactions on the site share one visual grammar and the form reads as three seconds of work rather than a chore. On the map, pins carry the price rather than a generic marker — a visitor scanning Salmiya sees the market, not a scatter of identical dots, which is the single most persuasive view a brokerage can show a seller deciding where to list.

What changed and why

On mobile the old build put the floating WhatsApp, call, and share buttons over the content as a stack of circles that covered listings while scrolling. Here the property page ends in a sticky footer that pairs the price with a single WhatsApp action — the two pieces of information a phone visitor acts on — and the floating circles are gone. 12 · Build

What this costs to implement

Reusable as-isNo change

Every API route, the cursor pagination, the bilingual translation tables, RBAC, the admin panel's data layer, and the smart-search relaxation logic are untouched. This is a presentation-layer rebuild — the Arabic/English routing and RTL infrastructure already exist and are the expensive part.

Actually newScope

A token file replacing the current cream/navy/gold theme, two licensed Arabic faces, a theme switch with pre-paint application, and a rewrite of roughly a dozen components — card, badge, field, dropdown, nav, footer, dimension row, search bar, filter bar, gallery, map pin, sticky CTA.
